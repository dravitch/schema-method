What does a successful project look like in SCHEMA?

Success is not uninterrupted execution. A healthy project starts from a bounded problem, exposes a weak hypothesis early, rebuilds without discarding useful assets, covers its blind spots and reduces risk before resources become commitments.

Start well

Bound the problem, the next decision and the evidence required before selecting more solution than necessary.

Fail early and usefully

Let a weak claim fail while the cost is small, and record exactly what the result changes.

Rebuild without erasing

Preserve useful assets and redirect them toward a trajectory the evidence can support.

Expose blind spots

Examine technical scope, operational use, authority, action and value before they become late surprises.

Commit with less risk

Increase resources only when the new knowledge justifies a clearer and more consequential action.

SCHEMA is a filter, not a destination. Its purpose is to preserve the best achievable relationship between what the team knows and the action or decision it is ready to support.

Different roles. Different tasks. One decision path.

Problem steward

Clarifies the problem, affected actor and observable outcome before a solution is assumed.

Evidence steward

States the useful property, its scope, the supporting observations and the next decisive test.

Operational steward

Names the intended user, decision window, action and consequences of error.

Authority steward

Connects mandate, accountability, procedure and the right to decide or act.

Value steward

Defines the result worth measuring and why it justifies the next allocation.

Integration steward

Keeps provenance, interfaces, unknowns and decisions visible across meetings.

A methodological breadcrumb trail. Each trace lets absent team members resume the reasoning without reconstructing the whole meeting and gradually feeds the decision record.

From weekly trace to decision record

A checkpoint is not a rewritten transcript. It captures the current claim, the evidence behind it, the unresolved point, the next bounded verification and the decision that verification is meant to inform.

This reduces repeated consolidation and interpretation. People who missed a meeting can recover the reasoning, contributors can correct the record and project management can coordinate work without having to reconstruct the knowledge state from scattered conversations.

Over time, these short traces become the project review naturally: a visible history of what the team learned, how the trajectory changed and why the next request for resources is justified.
